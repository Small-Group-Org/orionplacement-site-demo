What the role does

At a small or mid-size firm, an estate planning attorney owns client relationships from the first meeting to the signing. A typical week covers:

  • Meeting clients, learning their goals and recommending a plan.
  • Drafting wills, revocable trusts, powers of attorney and medical directives.
  • Running trust administrations and probate matters from intake to closing, usually with a paralegal.
  • Supervising signings and helping clients fund their trusts.
  • For senior attorneys: taxable estates (Forms 706 and 709), business succession and bringing in clients.

Hiring tips

  1. Publish the base band. Postings with a pay range filter out mismatched applicants and signal that the firm is straightforward. Use the band for your city and level.
  2. State the practice mix. "60% planning, 40% probate and administration" tells a candidate more than "estate planning practice".
  3. Ask about administrations, not only drafting. Candidates who run administrations end to end move a firm's capacity most, and are priced up one band.
  4. Ask every candidate the same questions. Score them on the rubric straight after the interview. The hiring kit has the questions, a scorecard and an offer-letter outline.
  5. Move within a week. Strong candidates usually have 2 to 6 weeks' notice and more than one conversation open.
