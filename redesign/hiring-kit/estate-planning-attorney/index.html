Part 1

Job description template

Written for a 4–7 year associate. Replace the highlighted parts, including the city and base band. The example band is from the Houston salary guide; use the band for your city and level.

Estate planning associate attorney (4–7 years)

[Your firm] is a [8]-attorney firm in [your city] focused on estate planning, probate and trust administration. We are hiring an associate to own client relationships from the first meeting to the signing.

What you will do

  • Meet clients, learn their goals and recommend a plan.
  • Draft wills, revocable trusts, powers of attorney and medical directives.
  • Run trust administrations and probate matters from intake to closing, with a paralegal supporting you.
  • Supervise signings and help clients fund their trusts.

What you bring

  • A [state] law license in good standing.
  • 4–7 years of estate planning practice.
  • Drafting you are confident to send without close review.
  • Ease leading client meetings on your own.
  • A plus: estate tax returns (Forms 706 and 709), Spanish.

Pay and benefits

  • Base salary [$115,000 to $150,000], depending on experience.
  • Health insurance, bar dues and CLE paid.

How we work

[On-site, Monday to Friday], in our [city] office.

How to apply

Send your resume to [hiring@yourfirm.com]. We reply to every applicant within five business days.

Hiring at 1–3 years? Use $90,000 to $115,000. At 8+ years, $150,000 to $195,000.

Part 2

Screening questions

Ask every candidate the same questions in the same order. Write down the specific matters they describe, then score them on the rubric straight after.

  1. Walk me through the last estate plan you designed, from the first meeting to the signing.

    Strong answer
    Names the client's goals, the documents chosen and why, who drafted what, and how signing and funding were handled.
    Red flag
    Talks only about filling in a template.

  2. When would you recommend a revocable trust over a will for a client in your state?

    Strong answer
    Mentions incapacity planning, privacy, property in other states and blended families. Says a will is often enough where the state's probate process is simple.
    Red flag
    Recommends a trust for everyone.

  3. Tell me about a trust administration you ran. What did you do in the first 30 days?

    Strong answer
    Notices to beneficiaries, a tax ID for the trust, an inventory, securing assets, a timeline for distributions.
    Red flag
    Cannot describe the steps in order.

  4. A client's adult children join the meeting and disagree with the plan. What do you do?

    Strong answer
    Identifies who the client is, meets the client alone, records capacity and intent, checks for conflicts.
    Red flag
    Tries to find a plan that pleases the children.

  5. How many plans did you draft a month, and how much did a partner review?

    Strong answer
    Gives concrete numbers and says honestly what review they still need.
    Red flag
    Vague about volume.

  6. A client's estate may be above the federal exemption. What do you raise?

    Strong answer
    Portability, lifetime gifts, Form 709 for gifts, Form 706 at death, and when to bring in a CPA.
    Red flag
    Has never worked on a taxable estate and does not say so.

  7. What would you need from us in your first 90 days?

    Strong answer
    Specific: templates, paralegal time, how client intake works, who reviews first drafts.
    Red flag
    No questions about how the firm runs.

Part 3

Scoring rubric

Score each criterion from 1 to 5: 1 unsatisfactory, 2 marginal, 3 competent, 4 advanced, 5 exceptional. Multiply each score by its weight and add them up for a total out of 5.0.

Estate planning attorney scoring rubric
CriterionWeightWhat a 5 looks likeScore (1–5)
Drafting quality25%Clean, accurate first drafts
Trust and probate administration20%Runs a matter end to end
Client meetings20%Leads a meeting alone and explains plainly
State estates law15%Knows your state's probate and trust code and local practice
File volume and organization10%Carries a full caseload
Fit with a small firm10%Comfortable with lean support

Example: scores of 4, 4, 5, 3, 4 and 5 give 4 × 0.25 + 4 × 0.20 + 5 × 0.20 + 3 × 0.15 + 4 × 0.10 + 5 × 0.10 = 4.15 out of 5.0. Many firms move to an offer at 3.8 or above with no criterion below 3; set the threshold that fits your firm.

Part 4

Offer-letter outline

Ten clauses an estate planning offer should cover. Have your employment counsel or managing partner review the final letter.

  1. Position and start date. Job title, the attorney they report to, the start date.
  2. Base salary. Annual base, pay schedule, when it is next reviewed.
  3. Bonus or origination. How any bonus is worked out and when it is paid; the share of fees on originated matters, if any.
  4. Benefits. Health insurance, bar dues, CLE, malpractice cover, paid time off.
  5. Work arrangement. On-site or hybrid days, office hours, where they work.
  6. License condition. The offer depends on an active license in your state, in good standing.
  7. At-will statement. Employment in most states is at-will unless you agree otherwise in writing. Check your state's rule.
  8. Confidentiality and client files. Client confidences, firm files and what happens to them when someone leaves.
  9. Restrictions after leaving. ABA Model Rule 5.6, and your state's version of it, limits agreements that restrict a lawyer's practice after leaving. Keep this section to what your state's rule allows.
  10. Acceptance. A date to reply by and a signature line.
